A noun clause is a dependent clause that acts as a noun.

<Callout title="Example">
  She loves **violet**. (noun)

  I know **that she loves violet**. (noun clause)
</Callout>

A noun clause can begin with words such as _what, who, when, where, whether, which, why, how, etc._

<Callout title="Example">
  I don't know **who called me**.

  It's important to state in your application **why you want to work at the company**.
</Callout>

A noun clause can act as subject, an object, or an object of a preposition.

<Callout title="Example">
  **Why he did this** was beyond my understanding. (subject)

  We would like to know **whether you see yourself coming back to our resort next year.** (object)

  She told us about **how she managed to get her intern position**. (object of a preposition)
</Callout>

## Short Story using Noun Clause

–_I wish I could grow up faster!_

–_Why?_

–_Well, I could do **whatever I wanted**, I could be **wherever I wanted**. No one would ask me **why I did something**._

–_Did you have a fight with your parents?_

–_No! I mean, sort of... They didn't like **that I came home past midnight**._

–_Of course they didn't like it! And I guess you didn't even call them saying **that you'll be that late**._
